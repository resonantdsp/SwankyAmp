# Factory voicing

Generated by `just refit`.

Version 2 discretises the tone stack with the standard bilinear constant
`2·SR`. Swanky Amp 1.4.0 used `SR`, which voiced every tone-stack
feature an octave above the circuit. The factory presets were made on
that stack, so each is revoiced to keep its character: the same
balance between bands at the output, and the power stage driven as
hard. The corrected stack cannot reproduce the octave-high scoop
exactly, so the aim is the right range, not a replica.

## Method

- Input: the two guitar recordings in `verification/reference/input`, single
coil and humbucker, played at Input 0 with `RECORDING_GAIN_DB` (2 dB)
applied to both.
- Each preset is rendered through 1.4.0 (the legacy path, which `just
model-check` holds to the released renders) and through the shipping
path at 44100 Hz with Auto oversampling.
- Balance is the output's sixth-octave band levels from 80 Hz to 16 kHz,
each render's bands taken about their own mean, so level does not
count. It is judged at the strikes: the loudest 40 % of each
recording's momentary-loudness blocks (the blocks the strike level
ranks, below), picked on the recording, where the player struck, so
every render is measured over the same stretches. That share takes in
the first plucks as well as the strum and the chord. The error is the
band difference from 1.4.0 averaged at the fourth power, so a narrow
peak counts, averaged over the recordings. The whole-recording error,
which the ring-out dominates, is reported beside it.
- Low, Mid, High, Presence and Power Drive sit on a grid of half marks
on the 0 to 10 scale. The tone controls start from the bank being
replaced, the voicing accepted by ear, on whichever neighbouring grid
values match best; among roundings within 0.1 dB of the best,
the one off the rails and nearest 1.4.0's settings. They then move a
step at a time while a step lowers the error by 0.1 dB or more,
and stay within 1 to 9, so presets leave room either way.
- Every candidate is judged with Power Drive set so the power stage's
input level, averaged over the recordings, matches 1.4.0's; where it
runs out of range, the miss costs 0.5 dB² per dB². The bank
then takes the grid value nearest that setting in gain.
- Output then brings each preset's strike level on the humbucker to
the level reference's, 1.4.0's defaults. The strike level is the 95th percentile of momentary
loudness (BS.1770-4 K-weighted 400 ms blocks at a 100 ms hop, those
above -70 LUFS) over the whole recording. Integrated loudness
averages over the ring-out, where a driven amp sustains and a clean
one decays, so a driven preset level with the reference on it strikes
softer. A clean amp follows the pickup and a driven one does not,
so on the single coil the driven presets come out louder than the reference.
